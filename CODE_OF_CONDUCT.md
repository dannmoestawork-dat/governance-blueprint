# Code of Conduct

This project works from a frame of **freedom of choice and autonomy**. A code of conduct can drift into a
rulebook that polices speech; that's not the goal here. The goal is a floor that lets people collaborate
without friction, while leaving everyone as much room as possible.

## The whole thing, briefly
- **Assume good faith.** Read the most charitable version of what someone wrote before responding.
- **Be straight, be kind.** Direct, honest, and respectful are not in tension. Critique ideas, not people.
- **Respect autonomy — yours and others'.** Disagree freely; you're never obligated to adopt anyone's view,
  and they're not obligated to adopt yours. "I'm going to fork and do it my way" is a legitimate, welcome
  answer, not a falling-out.
- **Leave it better.** Help newcomers when you can. You're not required to, but it's the spirit we like.

## What's actually out of bounds
A short floor, not a long ceiling. Not welcome here:
- Harassment, threats, or targeting people for who they are.
- Deliberate bad faith — doxxing, sustained disruption, posting others' private information.
- Using the project's spaces to harm people. Working definition of harm: Harm is the non-consensual violation of an individual's physical person or property, including explicit threats of physical violence, theft, or fraud that deliberately subverts consent.

Strong opinions, blunt technical disagreement, and "I think this is wrong because…" are all fine — encouraged,
even. The line is conduct that attacks *people*, not conduct that challenges *ideas*.

## If something goes wrong
We prefer the lightest resolution that works: most things are best handled by talking directly, in good faith.
If that isn't safe or hasn't worked, contact the maintainer privately:

> **Maintainer contact:** Dann Moesta — dann.moesta.work@gmail.com

Reports are handled discreetly and in good faith. The maintainer will use judgment proportionate to the
situation — a quiet word, a request to edit or step back, or, for clear bad-faith harm, removal. There's no
elaborate tribunal; there's a person trying to keep the space good while respecting everyone's autonomy.

## Scope
Applies in the project's own spaces (issues, PRs, discussions). What you do elsewhere is your business, unless
it's being used to harm participants here.
